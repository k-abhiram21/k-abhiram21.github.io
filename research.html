02 INTERNSHIP / LLMLENS / PHASE 1

CHARACTERIZING DETECTION LIMITS

How far does
uncertainty get us?

I wanted to understand the limits of uncertainty-based hallucination detection.

We studied whether signals from a model’s answers could distinguish correct responses from hallucinations, across different models and tasks.

Repeated answers can agree and still be wrong. I helped investigate where uncertainty signals are useful and where they stop separating the two.

MY CONTRIBUTION

I contributed to the research question, methodology, software, formal analysis, and manuscript writing with the team.

MANUSCRIPT · WITH EDITOR AT KAIS

Where Internal Signals Fail: Characterizing the Performance Ceiling of Uncertainty-Based Hallucination Detection

Submitted to Knowledge and Information Systems.

How I approached the investigation ↓

The question we studied.

I was interested in how much we can learn from a model’s uncertainty about its own answers. A detection method may work in one setting and become less informative in another. We wanted to understand that limit.

My work on the experiments.

I helped develop the methodology, wrote software, and worked on the analysis. We compared behaviour across different settings and used the results to examine the question. Debugging and interpreting the experiments were both part of that work.

Writing the paper.

I also worked on the manuscript: explaining why we asked the question, how we investigated it, and what the findings support. The paper is currently with an editor at Knowledge and Information Systems.

03 INTERNSHIP / MY ROUTING WORK / PHASE 2

MAKING MORE CONSIDERED MODEL DECISIONS

When should we use
a different answer?

My part of the project is routing: deciding when to use another model’s response.

The wider framework is a team effort. I work on the routing implementation, debugging, verification, and the tools we use to evaluate it.

I’m interested in whether the extra model helps with the task, and whether we can understand why the system made its choice.

MY CONTRIBUTION / ROUTING

Routing implementation, debugging, verification, and evaluation tooling within the team’s research framework.

FRAME

Define the choice.

Is another model’s answer useful for this task, rather than just relevant to its topic?

INVESTIGATE

Look at what happened.

Trace a decision through the implementation and work out why it behaved that way.

REFINE

Check the change.

Use the evaluation to see whether the next version addresses the problem.

How I shaped the routing question ↓

The routing question.

A model may recognise the topic without improving the answer. My work focuses on the decision to use its response. I want the routing behaviour to connect to what the task needs, rather than treating a plausible-looking answer as enough.

What I work on.

I implement and debug the routing code, work on verification, and build evaluation tools. That means moving between the research question and the implementation: reading what the code actually does, checking its behaviour, and working through changes with the team.

Working within the team.

Routing sits within a larger framework developed by the team. I need to understand the components around it to connect my work properly, while keeping my own responsibility clear. The broader research idea and framework are shared work.

What debugging taught me about model decisions ↓

When the output looks right.

A response can sound convincing without helping with the task. In the routing and verification work, I spend time looking at these cases and asking what led to the choice. Reading the output is a starting point; understanding the decision takes more investigation.

Working back through the code.

I trace the relevant implementation and check the assumptions around it. Is the code behaving as intended? Does the intended behaviour address the problem? Keeping those questions separate helps me decide whether I’m fixing a bug or revisiting an idea.

What that changes.

The debugging and verifier work feed back into the next iteration. They help me identify what needs another check and where the system’s behaviour is difficult to explain. I work with the team to turn those observations into changes we can evaluate.

How I make the work easier to evaluate ↓

What are we comparing?

I work on the evaluation code as well as the router. I need to understand what each comparison measures and how it relates to the question we started with. A number on its own doesn’t tell me whether the change did what we wanted.

Checking the evaluation code.

I debug the machinery we use to study model behaviour and tighten its checks. When a result looks unusual, that code is part of what needs investigating. An implementation issue can change what an experiment appears to show.

Looking at the added work.

Using another component has a cost as well as a possible benefit. I’m interested in whether it helps enough to justify the added work and how it fits with the rest of the system. That’s an engineering question alongside the research one.
