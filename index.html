Why the obvious choice can fail.

A model can know the subject and still give a poor answer. That’s part of what interests me about routing. My work involves looking at the choices the system makes, investigating the ones that look wrong, and checking whether a change actually helps with the task.

How I frame the question ↗

What I look for in a failure.

When the output isn’t what I expected, I go back through the implementation and the assumptions behind it. Sometimes the problem is in my code; sometimes the idea needs work. I want to understand which before adding another component or running another experiment.

Lessons from debugging ↗

Checking the experiment itself.

The evaluation code needs debugging too. I spend time checking what a comparison is measuring and whether it answers the question we started with. It’s easy to focus on a result and overlook the machinery that produced it; working on that machinery is part of my role.
